Our Research Projects

The Molecular Mechanism of Transmembrane Signal Transduction

We investigate the structural and molecular basis of transmembrane signal transduction using structural biology and biochemical approaches. Our research focuses on three major pathways: the cAMP signaling pathway, gap junction-mediated intercellular communication, and the Hedgehog signaling pathway.

Structural Characterization of Amyloid Fibrils from Human Brain

Using cryo-electron microscopy, we determine high-resolution structures of amyloid fibrils extracted directly from human brain tissues. Our goal is to understand the molecular architecture and structural diversity of pathological filaments associated with neurodegenerative diseases including Alzheimer's disease, frontotemporal dementia, and related tauopathies.

Neurodegenerative Disease Models

We develop and apply cellular and animal models to investigate the mechanisms underlying tau aggregation, propagation, and neurotoxicity. By integrating structural biology with disease models, we aim to uncover new therapeutic strategies for Alzheimer's disease and other neurodegenerative disorders.
